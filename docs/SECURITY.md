# Security model

**Status: unaudited, testnet only.** In scope: `contracts/src` (hub on Mezo: `VaultManager`, `PToken` / `MezodotPool`, `InterestRateModel`, `OracleRouter` with `PythSource` and `SupraSource`, `WormholeAdapter`, `LeverageManager`, `SwapLeverageManager`, `KeeperGateway`; spokes: `SpokeGateway`; the `Payload` codec). There are no proxies: every contract is immutable once deployed, and a change ships as a redeploy ([DEPLOY.md, Redeploy](DEPLOY.md#9-redeploy)). Operations: [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md); protocol revenue: [REVENUE.md](REVENUE.md).

## System in one paragraph

`VaultManager` on Mezo is the only ledger of positions: collateral, debt in each hub market (MUSD, MEZO), borrow limits (Σ value × LTV), health (Σ value × liquidation threshold / debt) and liquidations. Each market is a `PToken` (pMUSD, pMEZO): an ERC-4626 vault that is also the lending pool (borrow index, kinked rates, reserves, ERC-3156 flash loans). Collateral comes in three kinds: **share** (pMUSD/pMEZO escrowed in `VaultManager`, valued through the pool's exchange rate), **plain** (Mezo's BTC, unit for unit) and **remote** (WETH/USDC locked in a `SpokeGateway` on Base Sepolia or Ethereum Sepolia and credited on the hub by a verified Wormhole DEPOSIT; withdrawals and seizures leave as a RELEASE). `LeverageManager` (MUSD loop) and `SwapLeverageManager` (BTC long through Tigris) are stateless operators that users authorise with `VaultManager.setOperator`.

### Admin powers (testnet: the deployer EOA)

These can move or freeze user funds. A production deployment must put them behind a timelock and a multisig, with a guardian role that can only tighten parameters. `pnpm check:admin-docs` (CI) fails when an `onlyOwner` function in `contracts/src` is missing from this table.

### Pause semantics

Pausing never blocks exits or risk reduction, and it is not an oracle or bridge switch.

| Contract                 | `pause` blocks                                                                                                                        | Stays open                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `VaultManager`           | `borrow`, `borrowFor` (so `LeverageManager.loop` and `SwapLeverageManager.open` revert)                                               | Collateral deposits and withdrawals, `withdrawRemote`, `repay`, `repayWithCollateral`, `liquidate`, `creditRemote` (DEPOSIT credits) |
| `PToken` (`MezodotPool`) | `deposit`, `mint`, pool `borrow`, `flashLoan`                                                                                         | `withdraw`, `redeem`, repayments, `repayWithShares`, write-offs, `withdrawReserves`                                                  |
| `SpokeGateway`           | `deposit`                                                                                                                             | `receiveMessage` (RELEASEs), `claim`                                                                                                 |
| Not pausable             | `OracleRouter.updatePrices`, `WormholeAdapter.receiveMessage`, `KeeperGateway`, `SwapLeverageManager.close`, `LeverageManager.unwind` | —                                                                                                                                    |

So a pause stops new risk but not liquidations at a wrong price, nor credits from a forged VAA. Those levers are `setSource` / `setFeed` (stop or repoint a price) and `setSpoke` / `setHubEmitter` (cut the bridge); [RUNBOOK.md](RUNBOOK.md) has the procedures. `contracts/script/Pause.s.sol` pauses or unpauses the hub (VaultManager and both pools) or one spoke.

## Oracle design

`OracleRouter` gives each asset a primary and an optional secondary leg, each with its own max age, plus a per-feed confidence cap and cross-check limit (deployed configuration, checked by `pnpm verify:deployment`):

| Key  | Primary (Pyth)                               | Secondary (Supra) | Confidence cap | Cross-check |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------- | ----------- |
| MUSD | push, 3,900 s                                | —                 | 5%             | —           |
| MEZO | push, 3,900 s                                | —                 | 5%             | —           |
| USDC | push, 3,900 s                                | pair 89, 120 s    | 2%             | 1.5%        |
| ETH  | 120 s (pull only: ETH is not pushed on Mezo) | pair 19, 120 s    | 2%             | 1.5%        |
| BTC  | push, 3,900 s                                | pair 18, 120 s    | 2%             | 1.5%        |

- **A leg is usable** when its source returns a positive price no older than the leg's max age (a publish time ahead of the block counts as age 0; Supra accepts at most 15 s ahead) and its confidence is within the cap. A source that reverts counts as no data, so a broken vendor contract cannot block the other leg.
- **Freshest leg wins.** Strict reads serve the usable leg with the newer publish time (the primary on a tie), so an hour-old push never overrides a seconds-old pull proof.
- **Cross-check, fail closed.** The other leg must agree within the cross-check limit when both legs are usable and were published within 60 s of each other (`CROSS_CHECK_WINDOW`), or when the other leg holds _newer_ data that failed its own freshness or confidence check: an older price is never served against newer information that contradicts it. Disagreement reverts strict reads with `PriceDiverged` and makes `tryGetPrice` / `getPriceInfo` report the key unavailable.
- `getPriceInfo` returns the selected leg's expiry as `validUntil`; the other leg may still serve afterwards, subject to the cross-check. Clients attach a pull update only when a key would expire before their transaction lands.
- **Valuation is conservative:** debt is priced strictly (missing price ⇒ revert); collateral without an acceptable price counts as zero toward borrow and withdraw limits; liquidation prices every held asset strictly, so an outage cannot make a healthy position liquidatable. Debt-free accounts withdraw without any price.
- The 3,900 s max age covers Mezo's hourly push (longest gap seen since the cutover: 3,668 s). The 5% caps for MUSD/MEZO fit the measured distributions (MUSD conf/price: median 1.22%, p99 2.87%); a 2% cap froze MUSD for hours at a time.
- Price updates are `sourceTag (1 byte) ‖ vendor payload` (Pyth = 1, Supra = 2). `SupraSource` caches only proofs newer than the stored value per pair, and Pyth keeps only newer prices, so no update can roll a price back.

## Leverage

### MUSD loop (`LeverageManager`)

- `loop` borrows the extra exposure as a pool flash loan (0.09% fee, to reserves), supplies principal + loan, posts the pMUSD and borrows `amount + fee` against it to repay the flash loan, all checked by `VaultManager` (an over-levered loop reverts). Requires `VaultManager.setOperator(LeverageManager, true)` and an MUSD approval.
- `unwind` nets the debt against the posted pMUSD with `VaultManager.repayWithCollateral`: no flash loan, no second fee, no idle cash needed, so it works at any utilisation. It then withdraws `shares` of what is left (max = all) and redeems what idle cash allows; the rest is returned as pMUSD. `minOut` counts returned pMUSD at its redemption value. A full unwind needs no price update.
- Supply and debt are in the same pool, so the carry is negative by construction (suppliers earn borrow rate × utilisation × (1 − reserve factor)): the loop exists for transparent leverage, not yield.

### BTC long (`SwapLeverageManager`)

A flash swap on the Tigris MUSD/BTC pool replaces the MUSD pool's flash loan:

- `open(principal, borrowAmount, minBought, deadline, priceUpdate)`: takes the principal, quotes `bought = pair.getAmountOut(borrowAmount, MUSD)` (must be ≥ `minBought`), and asks the pool for `bought` BTC. In the callback it posts principal + bought as the caller's collateral and borrows exactly `borrowAmount` MUSD straight to the pool as payment; the pool then checks its invariant. Debt = `borrowAmount` exactly: no flash fee. It needs pMUSD idle cash ≥ `borrowAmount` (1×, not 2×).
- `close(repayAmount, withdrawAmount, sellAmount, minProceeds, deadline, priceUpdate)`: clamps `repayAmount` to the live debt (`max` = all of it), quotes `proceeds = pair.getAmountOut(sellAmount, BTC)` (must be ≥ `repayAmount` and ≥ `minProceeds`), takes the MUSD from the pool, repays, withdraws `withdrawAmount` (`max` = all) and pays the pool `sellAmount` BTC. Unsold BTC and the MUSD beyond the repayment go to the caller. Close needs no MUSD pool cash at all; a full close needs no price update.
- The callback only runs inside the manager's own swap (`_inSwap` flag, `msg.sender == pair`, `sender == this`); both entry points are `nonReentrant`. The constructor checks that the ids name the pool's market and a plain hub collateral, and that the pair holds exactly MUSD and BTC. The manager holds nothing between transactions.
- A one-hop router quote (`getAmountsOut`) equals `pair.getAmountOut`, so apps compute `minBought` / `minProceeds` from the router and the contract executes the pool's own quote.

### Repay with collateral

`VaultManager.repayWithCollateral(account, marketId, collateralId, amount)` (the account or its operator) repays debt by burning the account's posted shares of the same pool, like Aave's `repayWithATokens`: `PToken.repayWithShares` burns shares worth at least the debt cleared (rounded up, so the exchange rate never falls) and shrinks supply and borrows together. No cash moves and no price is read: debt and collateral value fall by the same amount `x` (the pMUSD collateral and the MUSD market share a price key), so `limit − debt` grows by about `x × (1 − LTV)` and `adjusted collateral − debt` by about `x × (1 − LT)`: a position only moves away from liquidation. `amount = type(uint256).max` repays as much as the posted shares cover. It reverts if it would leave debt with no collateral at all (`WouldLeaveBadDebt`) and emits `RepaidWithCollateral`.

## Protocol revenue

Reserve factor on interest (default 10%, owner-settable up to 30%) and the 0.09% flash fee accrue to each pool's reserves; 10% of every liquidation bonus (owner-settable up to 50%) is credited to the treasury account as collateral in `VaultManager` custody. Details, reporting and collection: [REVENUE.md](REVENUE.md).

## Key invariants (tested)

- **Pool solvency:** `cash + totalBorrows ≥ totalReserves`; per-account debts sum to `totalBorrows` (up to rounding dust); escrowed shares match the ledger (`PoolInvariant`).
- **Cross-chain backing:** hub credit per (spoke, token) ≤ tokens locked in that SpokeGateway, including while RELEASE messages are in flight (fuzz + `CrossChainInvariant`). Deposits lock before they credit; withdrawals and liquidations debit the hub before the spoke releases. A RELEASE whose token transfer fails is kept as `claimable[recipient][token]` (`ReleaseDeferred`, then `claim(token, to)`), so the hub debit is never lost.
- **Whole hub (`HubInvariant`, with the real adapter, a non-zero hub Wormhole fee, BTC, the Tigris pool, both leverage managers, the keeper gateway and the treasury fee):** escrow equals recorded totals for every local collateral; no account owes anything, not even dust, without collateral; supplier share prices fall only across a bad-debt write-off; reserves equal the reserve-factor share of interest plus flash fees, less withdrawals and absorbed bad debt; the leverage managers, `KeeperGateway` and the adapter hold nothing between transactions; the Tigris pool's k never decreases.
- **Message binding (payload v2):** every message names its destination (Wormhole chain id + contract). A VAA cannot be executed by a redeployed gateway, another spoke, or a new hub adapter, and each VAA hash is consumed once by the only contract it names. Message type is checked per direction; only the registered emitter per chain is accepted.
- **One-shot adapter:** `setAdapter` cannot be called twice, so the DEPOSIT replay guard (held by the adapter) cannot be reset.
- **Recipients:** withdrawals and remote liquidations only accept left-padded EVM addresses, so released collateral cannot be frozen on a spoke.
- **Flash-loan share price:** `totalAssets` counts the outstanding flash amount, so deposits during a flash loan mint fair shares (a mutation test showed a 10× share gain without it).
- **ERC-4626 inflation:** 6-decimal virtual share offset + seed deposit.
- **Borrow limit:** every new borrow and every collateral withdrawal is checked against Σ value × LTV, with debt valued rounding up (a few wei of a sub-dollar token never count as no debt); liquidation only below HF 1, 50% close factor, `minSeize` guard (after the protocol's fee).
- **Native BTC is never spent on fees:** `VaultManager` holds BTC collateral as its native balance, so `withdrawRemote` and remote liquidations check that `msg.value` covers the price updates plus the Wormhole fee before the RELEASE is paid for (`MessageFee` tests); every risk-checked entry point refunds unused `msg.value`.
- **Bad debt:** when a liquidation seizes an account's last collateral, the remaining debt is written off: reserves absorb it first, the rest lowers the pToken exchange rate pro rata.
- **Leverage:** BTC opens carry debt equal to the borrow, closes never repay more than the live debt (nothing stranded), and both callbacks reject foreign callers; loops revert above the LTV, unwinds work with no idle cash.
- **Oracle:** the freshest usable leg is served; an older price contradicted by newer data fails closed; stale or low-confidence legs are ignored.

## Known limitations

- **MUSD and MEZO have a single price source:** Pyth, pushed by Mezo about hourly, with no keyless secondary. The agent's Pyth pusher helps only with a Pyth key _entitled to those feeds_; the key in use today is entitled to ETH and BTC but not MUSD or MEZO (HTTP 403), so Mezo's pusher is the only source. With a 3,900 s max age and an hourly push there are about 5 minutes of slack: if Mezo's push is later than that, borrows, withdrawals with debt, liquidations touching those assets, BTC leverage opens and partial closes, and MUSD loop opens revert until someone pushes ([RUNBOOK, oracle outage](RUNBOOK.md#1-oracle-outage)). Repayments, full closes, full unwinds and debt-free withdrawals keep working. Within the window, prices can lag by up to the pusher's deviation trigger (1–2%).
- **ETH prices need a proof per transaction.** The web app gets Supra proofs through the agent's `/oracle/update` relay (Supra's endpoint rejects browser CORS). If Supra or the agent is down, WETH counts as $0 toward borrowing, and liquidations of WETH-backed positions wait for a proof. BTC falls back the same way when its hourly push is stale.
- **Pull-price choice:** within the 120 s window a caller may pick which valid proof to submit (bounded by the cross-check against newer data).
- **BTC is the gas balance.** BTC collateral comes out of the balance that pays gas, so "max" deposits must leave a reserve (the web app does). BTC sent to a contract by ERC-20 transfer cannot be refused: stray BTC in `KeeperGateway` goes to the next liquidator; stray BTC in the leverage managers stays there (they never sweep).
- **DEX price vs oracle price.** BTC leverage trades at the Tigris pool price while positions are valued at the oracle price: buying BTC above the oracle price costs equity at once, and `minBought` / `minProceeds` bound slippage against the quote, not against the oracle. A thin or manipulated pool makes opens expensive and the agent's sales of seized BTC worse (`MAX_SALE_DISCOUNT_BPS` bounds them). If the pool stops working, opens and closes revert; positions can still be exited with `repay` + `withdrawCollateral`.
- **BTC leverage capacity:** an open borrows from pMUSD's idle cash, so at full utilisation no new BTC positions open (closes are unaffected).
- **Relayer liveness:** if no one relays, deposits are not credited and releases are not executed (funds are safe; anyone, including the user from the UI, can relay).
- **Hub account = spoke address:** the depositor's address on the spoke is their hub account. Smart-contract wallets with different addresses per chain should not post cross-chain collateral.
- **Treasury fees are custody balances:** liquidation fees sit in `VaultManager` as the treasury account's collateral (remote WETH/USDC become tokens only after `withdrawRemote` and a RELEASE). They carry the same price exposure as any collateral, and would back the treasury's debt if it ever borrowed.
- **Dust:** an account left with dust collateral keeps its debt until that dust is liquidated; the aggregate borrow total can retain a few thousand wei after all debts are repaid (Compound-style rounding).
- **Disabled collateral still counts for health:** `enabled=false` stops new borrowing against an asset without making positions liquidatable; it does not force exits.
- **Precompiles in tooling:** forge cannot execute MEZO or BTC locally, so the hub is broadcast with `--skip-simulation` (see DEPLOY.md), and fork tests cannot exercise BTC (`decimals()` reverts on an anvil fork). The real BTC path is covered by `scripts/smoke-btc.sh` on testnet after each deploy.
- **Single owner key:** one EOA owns every contract on three chains and is the treasury. Until a timelock and multisig take over, key hygiene is the control ([DEPLOY.md, key storage](DEPLOY.md#key-storage)).
